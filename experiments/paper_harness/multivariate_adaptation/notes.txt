Multivariate distribution adaptation validation
===============================================

Purpose
-------
Test whether robust marginal scaling, covariance shrinkage, chronological
normal-block calibration, and explicit multivariate denial improve transfer on
the same TEP, PRONTO, and SKAB partitions used by the frozen Book Chapter 3
harness.

Variants
--------
M0: frozen classical Mahalanobis baseline from the Chapter 3 protocol.
M1: median/MAD scaling plus 0.1 ridge covariance shrinkage.
M2: M1 plus recent-reference and chronological normal-block threshold/delay.
M3: calibration-only static/adapt/reject router; selective results always report
coverage and denial counts.

Boundaries
----------
Feature selection reads normal training and abnormal calibration only. Held-out
normal and abnormal partitions are used for post-hoc distribution diagnosis and
scoring, never routing or threshold selection. Moving-block bootstrap resamples
normal and abnormal evaluation partitions separately. These are engineering
transfer results, not reproduction of the five original Chapter 3 paper scores.

Observed result
---------------
The aggregate M0 F1 values are 0.8921/0.7229/0.1049 on TEP/PRONTO/SKAB.
M2 falls to 0.8351/0.5640/0.0984 and increases FAR on all three datasets.
Robust scaling and covariance shrinkage therefore do not solve operating-regime
shift. M3 denies all three TEP d11 seed units and has 0.667 TEP coverage, but it
cannot anticipate future PRONTO/SKAB normal-regime drift from calibration data.
Do not promote M1-M3 as a general improvement.
